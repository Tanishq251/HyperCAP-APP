HyperCap: Hyperspectral Land Cover Captioning Dataset for Vision Language Models

Abstract

We introduce HyperCap, the first large-scale hyperspectral captioning dataset designed to enhance model performance and effectiveness in remote sensing applications. Unlike traditional hyperspectral imaging (HSI) benchmarks, HyperCap integrates spectral data with pixel-wise textual annotations, enabling deeper semantic understanding. This dataset enhances model performance in tasks like classification and feature extraction, providing a valuable resource for advanced remote sensing applications. HyperCap is constructed from four benchmark datasets and annotated through a hybrid approach combining automated and manual methods to ensure accuracy and consistency. Empirical evaluations using state-of-the-art encoders and diverse fusion techniques demonstrate significant improvements in classification performance. These results underscore the potential of vision-language learning in HSI and position HyperCap as a foundational dataset for future research in the field.

Highlights

1
First pixel-level HSI captioning dataset. HyperCap provides fine-grained, pixel-wise textual descriptions for four widely-used hyperspectral benchmarks — Botswana, Houston 2013, Indian Pines, and Kennedy Space Center — totaling 21,237 expert-refined captions. No prior dataset achieves this level of spectral–semantic granularity.
2
Hybrid annotation pipeline with expert quality control. Captions were generated in parallel by ChatGPT-4o and Mistral Large using a structured prompt, then reviewed and refined by three remote sensing experts. Constraints prohibited class-name use, functional language, and speculative content — ensuring physically grounded, class-leakage-free descriptions.
3
Large classification gains across architectures. Integrating HyperCap captions via text encoders (BERT-Large, T5) boosted overall accuracy by up to +27% on Kennedy Space Center (DBCTNet: 70.5% → 97.6%) and +23% on Indian Pines (DBCTNet: 76% → 99.4%). Even top-performing models like FAHM reached 100% OA with textual fusion.
4
Robust under severe data scarcity. With only 3% of labeled pixels for training, multimodal models fusing HyperCap captions maintained above 98% OA — while vision-only baselines dropped 4–5%. This demonstrates that caption-guided supervision provides unique resilience when ground-truth annotations are scarce.
5
No label leakage confirmed. Ablation studies on text-only versus multimodal inputs show consistent and substantial performance gaps, ruling out captions as hidden class-label proxies. Captions genuinely encode complementary spectral–spatial semantics rather than target-label information.
6
Enables new multimodal tasks in HSI. Beyond classification, HyperCap supports image captioning and image-text retrieval benchmarks. GIT and mPLUG emerge as leading captioners, while retrieval results (IR: ~62%, TR: ~70%) indicate significant headroom for domain-adapted HSI vision-language architectures.
